# Shared writing rules

Every text others will read is checked against this file and the project's own `style/writing-rules.md` (its
audience, structure, voice and extra words). Merged from two projects' rules (2026-11-12, SU-Q27), which
were based on the Humanizer skill (github.com/blader/humanizer) and the user's feedback. `check_text.py` reads the
"Never use" section of both files.

## Dashes (hard rule, no exceptions)
- No em dashes and no en dashes anywhere: not in titles, tables, ranges or Russian/Greek text.
  The user: "That's a dead giveaway that it was written by AI."
- Replace each one with a period, comma, colon or parentheses, or rewrite the sentence.
  Russian copula dash ("Рядом с парком — библиотека") becomes a verb: "Рядом с парком находится библиотека".
- Number ranges use a plain hyphen or "to": 2-3 cars, "€40 to €60".
- Empty table cells stay empty; never fill them with a dash.
- Hyphens: before a noun ("a 2-hour walk"), not after it ("the walk takes 2 hours").

## Structure
- Lead with what the reader needs: the decision, the offer or the answer, then the reasons.
- List items start with the action or the fact ("Send the signed form by Friday."), not a bold label.
- Every risk comes with what to do about it.

## Voice
- Plain, specific, confident. Say what is there, with the number or the name.
- Vary sentence length. Paragraphs of different sizes, not all the same.
- British spelling in English (neighbourhood, metres, colour).
- Numbers as figures: 3 rooms, 24 m², €120.
- Use is, are, has. Not "serves as", "boasts", "features", "offers".

## Never use
nestled, boasts, stunning, breathtaking, exquisite, unparalleled, unrivalled, a testament to, seamlessly, elevate,
meticulously, curated, vibrant, bustling, embark, delve, journey, tapestry, crucial, pivotal, showcase, underscore,
renowned, at its core, the real question is, let's dive in, here's what you need to know, honestly, look no further,
don't miss out.

## Patterns to avoid
- "Not X but Y" / "it's not just X, it's Y" contrasts. State the point.
- One-line closers and dramatic fragments. End on the last concrete fact or the action.
- Forced triads. Keep three only if all three are true and distinct.
- Several sentences in a row starting the same way.
- Inflated significance. Keep the fact, drop the significance.
- Unnamed authority ("experts say", "industry reports"). Name the source or cut it.
- Bold scattered through prose.
- Title Case Headings. Use sentence case.
- Opening with a rhetorical question, "Welcome to..." or "We are pleased to announce".
- Writing about the document ("This report will outline..."). Just say it.
- Claims we cannot back: a view, a distance, a quality nobody measured or said.

## Russian / Greek
- Write natively in each language; do not translate sentence by sentence from English.
- Formal register where the project says so (Greek polite plural Σας/Εσείς).
- The user proofreads Russian and Greek text before anything is sent or published.
