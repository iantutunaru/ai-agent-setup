# Web design rulebook: colour

91 entries: 1 rule, 90 practice. Built by a script from the owner's reading notes, which are not published; not edited by hand. How to read an entry: [README.md](README.md).

### colour-001 · Give every colour a stated reason
- **Statement:** Choose each colour for what it has to communicate for the job, with a reason that can be stated, not because the designer or the client likes it. Using too many colours is a beginner's mistake; colours picked by method tell a single story together with the brand, the concept and the style of the site.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** BOULTON-097 (page 79), GENTRY-083 (page 106)
- **Also in (not read):** BOULTON-091, BEAIRD-085
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate colour E01

### colour-002 · Choose a colour whose associations fit what the brand stands for
- **Statement:** Align a brand or design with a colour whose meaning fits what it stands for, so the colour's associations reinforce the message. Blue feels calm and, as navy, traditional; it pairs well with white and many palettes, and combined with grey it evokes trust.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** BOULTON-120 (page 91), BOULTON-109 (page 85)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate trust E61

### colour-003 · Fit the palette to the subject's tone and the users' situation
- **Statement:** Colours should support the site's concept and the tone of its subject, or at least not conflict with them: for a solemn subject such as a war memorial bright happy colours feel out of place, and a water theme sits badly with lots of red. The palette can also come from an emotional portrait of the users: Wufoo took its palette's inspiration from competitors whose software reminded people they were making databases in a windowless office, and went the opposite way, bringing warmth and a little fun to cold, grey cubicles.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** GENTRY-084 (page 106), WALTER-008 (page 19)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate colour E02

### colour-004 · Check colour meanings against the audience's culture
- **Statement:** Treat the emotional meaning of a colour as a starting point only and check it against the culture of the target audience: red is hot and angry in the West but stands for wealth, purity and good fortune in the East, and black is associated with mourning in the West while in the East white is sometimes the colour of mourning. Many reactions to colour also come from personal experience.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** BEAIRD-086 (page 65-66), BOULTON-105 (page 83)
- **Also in (not read):** BEAIRD-094

### colour-006 · Keep the client's brand colours
- **Statement:** Respect the client's brand guidelines and signature colours, and preferably keep a logo's recognised brand colours unchanged on the site. When the site experience is far removed from the brand other colours can be used, but clear it first with the creative director or the client. Most logos, however, tolerate recolouring and can be fitted into a layout with any dominant colours.

### colour-008 · Build the palette on a colour-wheel scheme
- **Statement:** Build the palette on a colour-theory scheme (one colour on black and white, monochrome, analogous, complementary, split complementary, triad): colours selected from the wheel in certain combinations interact, knowing these combination types is essential to creating palettes, and a palette rooted in colour theory gives the design more structure and impact. Fit the scheme to the chosen visual style.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** GENTRY-086 (page 106), BOULTON-093 (page 74)
- **Also in (not read):** BEAIRD-098, BEAIRD-119
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate colour E07

### colour-009 · Treat the classic schemes as starting points to vary
- **Statement:** Colours picked at random from the wheel tend to give awful combinations; vary the classic schemes instead: add pure grey, black and white to a monochromatic scheme, raise the saturation of one colour while muting the others, add tints and shades of the base to a split-complementary scheme, or nudge a colour off its theoretical value (a green with some blue in it) to fit the mood the subject needs.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** BEAIRD-120 (page 91), GENTRY-096 (page 122)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate colour E08

### colour-010 · A quick guide from the concept to a scheme
- **Statement:** A quick guide from the site's concept to a scheme: friendly, analogous; hip or fun, split complementary; bold, complementary (but not too much colour, or it overpowers the page); grounded, triad. The colours are then tweaked from that starting palette.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** GENTRY-101 (page 129)
- **Conflict:** BOULTON-095 (in colour-026) says a triad creates tension and clash, not a grounded effect; BEAIRD-118 (in colour-024) says a split complementary scheme can look juvenile and extreme.
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate colour E10

### colour-011 · A one-hue scheme needs contrast between its steps
- **Statement:** A scheme of one base colour with its tints and shades can work (a light tint for the background, a darker one for text, a more saturated one for decoration), and a super-saturated hue such as hot pink packs a powerful punch paired with black and white. Colours that are close yet not identical irritate and too little contrast is dull, so with little experience use as few colours as possible but make them contrast.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BEAIRD-106 (page 78), KIRSANOV-134 (page 50)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate colour E11

### colour-012 · Black, white and one highlight colour
- **Statement:** Black and white plus one highlight colour is a simple, effective scheme: it can make dissimilar images look like one set and bring attention to colours that matter to the concept. In an achromatic scheme (black, white and greys only) a little colour stands out strongly: on The Climate Reality Project's dark grey map page bright yellow dots leap out against the grey, and the same yellow is used for the outline and label of the single button and for the visitor counter figure.

### colour-016 · Dark text on a light background for long reading
- **Statement:** Dark text on a light background is the better combination for reading: in a study reported by NN/g (Piepenbrock and others, Ergonomics 2013) it gave better visual acuity and proofreading than light on dark for younger and older adults. Other schemes with good legibility are fine where looks matter.| conflict: the same study found no difference in fatigue between the two polarities, against the book's "most comfortable" (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/)
- **Kind:** rule
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** KIRSANOV-142 (page 51)
- **Study (as the book reports it):** tests by psychologists (not named) point to black on white as the combination giving the most comfort in long reading, as Kirsanov reports them
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/ (read 2026-11-20)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate type E138

### colour-017 · An analogous scheme: neighbours on the wheel, one dominant
- **Statement:** An analogous scheme uses colours near each other on the wheel, as a rule of thumb spanning no more than one third of it; it works best with three or four colours, often with one dominant colour the others defer to, and suits a harmonious mood and friendly content.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** BEAIRD-111 (page 81), GENTRY-090 (page 111)
- **Also in (not read):** GENTRY-155
- **Conflict:** KIRSANOV-135 (in the Taste list) says never to pair hues that sit close together on the wheel, because the clash hurts the eye.
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate colour E17

### colour-018 · Use complementary colours with caution
- **Statement:** Complementary colours (opposite on the wheel) add vibrancy and a lot of tension and draw the eye, and a hue of the complement works well as a highlight colour. Use them with caution: large areas of them can hinder communication, some pairs simply look horrible while others are just right, and a muted pair can work because the colours are not too intense.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** GENTRY-092 (page 115-116, the notes cite 115), BOULTON-094 (page 75)
- **Also in (not read):** BEAIRD-114, BOULTON-184, KIRSANOV-498
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate colour E18

### colour-020 · Complementary colours as text on background are painful to look at
- **Statement:** Placed together, complementary colours make each other look more vivid (simultaneous contrast), which moves the eye around a layout; but used as foreground on background, such as text of one on a field of the other, they can be horribly painful to look at.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BEAIRD-116 (page 87)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate colour E20

### colour-021 · Check text and background pairs with a contrast checker
- **Statement:** Check foreground and background pairs with a contrast checker, such as the contrast section of Chrome DevTools' Color Picker, because combinations that look fine can fail the contrast WCAG sets: a contrast ratio from relative luminance of at least 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for large text (SC 1.4.3). Treat the tool as a guide rather than the final word; WCAG notes that anti-aliasing can make thin text fainter than its nominal ratio.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BEAIRD-125 (page 97)
- **Asked of the source:** the contrast measure and thresholds WCAG now sets, and a current checker (the book names Jonathan Snook's 2014 checker and its colour difference and brightness test).
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html (read 2026-11-20)
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://developer.chrome.com/docs/devtools/accessibility/contrast (read 2026-11-20)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate accessibility E73

### colour-024 · Split complementary can look juvenile and extreme
- **Statement:** A split-complementary scheme uses the base colour plus the two neighbours of its complement; because these are discordant with the base, the result can look juvenile and extreme, which may be just the effect wanted.

### colour-025 · Discordant colours: for the young, or sparingly for emphasis
- **Statement:** Colours that are neither opposite nor close enough to be analogous tend to clash (discordant colours); used on purpose they appeal to children and teenagers, and used sparingly in adult designs they give stronger emphasis than a plain complementary pair.

### colour-026 · A triad creates tension

### colour-048 · Three roles: subordinate, dominant, accent
- **Statement:** Structure a combination as three roles: a visually weak subordinate or base colour, which should contrast or complement; a dominant or main colour, which defines the combination's communicative values; and an accent or highlight colour, which either harmonises with the base or dominant colour or is visually strong and striking, competing with the dominant to add tension.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BOULTON-099 (page 80)
- **Also in (not read):** GENTRY-163
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate colour E47

### colour-051 · Logo colours as accent, quiet colours for information pages
- **Statement:** An information part of a page needs at least three colours, counting background and black. In his own site Kirsanov found the strong, tightly linked logo colours too active to play a part in the information pages, so outside the splash page they serve only as accent; he added a lighter shade of the background colour for links, rules and headings, which gave a system in which every colour has its role: a saturated contrasting pair in the logo and a pair of same-hue, low-contrast, low-saturation information colours.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** KIRSANOV-513 (page 157), KIRSANOV-514 (page 157)
