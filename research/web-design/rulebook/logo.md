### logo-001 · A logo is drawn, not typed
- **Statement:** Words merely set in a font, not unified and with nothing unique about them, are type, not a logo; a company that wants an exclusive mark does not use a font anybody can buy for a few dollars but has the letters drawn or customised. Such an arrangement might have been rescued by framing the words in a border or by enlarging the initial capitals.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** CABARGA-001 (page 4), CABARGA-053 (page 39)

### logo-002 · A logo reads at a glance
- **Statement:** A logo must never confuse a potential customer and must get its message across instantly at a glance; one whose design is too clever or too difficult to decipher is a failure.

### logo-004 · A logo that says what the company does
- **Statement:** A logo should provoke further investigation, saying the commonplace in an uncommon way (the author quotes Paul Rand), and its icon should say something about the nature of the company: an icon that tells nothing about it, beside a very boring font, neither intrigues nor informs. In one logo a hole and tag string added to a plain mark were just enough graphic context to show the company's purpose and catch the viewer's curiosity.

### logo-007 · A drawing set with lettering matches its line weight
- **Statement:** To make artwork sit with the text, illustrator Elliot Banfield, as the author reports, held that the finest lines of any artwork beside the text should be as thin as the finest strokes of the smallest type on the page. All the parts of a logo should be comprehensible at one glance without moving closer, for legibility, and because combining elements of very different line density (bold lettering with a fine-line illustration) wrecks the even colour of the piece.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** CABARGA-094 (page 119), CABARGA-101 (page 122)

### logo-008 · The typeface suits the shape of the mark
- **Statement:** Choose a logo typeface that supports the shape of the mark. A sans serif reads well small and pairs well with almost any graphic; a simple serif face is acceptable but demands a mark without complex curves or fine detail. In Kirsanov's Quiotix logo a serif face would have added horizontals running close and parallel to the mark's edges, and a monoline sans suited a mark of narrow necks and swellings poorly, so he chose the flared Optima. The reverse also works: a mark drawn to share a detail of the typeface.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** KIRSANOV-528 (page 162), KIRSANOV-447 (page 136)
- **Also in (not read):** KIRSANOV-569
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate type E231

### logo-009 · One typeface in a logo with a mark
- **Statement:** A logo with a graphic sign uses only one typeface, since a logo must be whole and simple; contrast parts of the name by colour or by the weights of that face, most often a regular and a bold of one sans serif. In one logo the light and bold weights of one family put the bold on the word that names the service. Two contrasting typefaces suit only a pure text logo.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** KIRSANOV-446 (page 135-136, the notes cite 136), BEAIRD-295 (page 169)

### logo-018 · No slivers of shape where elements meet
- **Statement:** Carelessly placed letters and shapes in a logo hurt legibility and look cluttered: avoid tiny slivers of shape sticking out where elements nearly meet, neither here nor there, and rearrange the elements to remove such poor juxtapositions. Where one shape must cross another, cross it through the middle, or at any rate well away from its edges, so the two strokes do not appear to merge.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** CABARGA-098 (page 122), CABARGA-099 (page 122)

### logo-019 · A logotype belongs to a wider visual language
- **Statement:** A logotype works as one element of a wider graphic language, together with colours, patterns, icons, signage parts and chosen typefaces, and it lives, and changes, on the page, the screen and in buildings. A bakery's branding develops its logotype together with three colour swatches (a dark blue, a lighter blue and a light grey), two sans serif alphabets (one condensed, one wider) and a dotted ornamental pattern, which work together to express the brand's personality. A Mexican bank's identity sheet sets out its custom typeface's character set with accented capitals, the logotype at five stepped sizes, the logo in its two colours over many backgrounds, and sub-brand names built in the same stacked form.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** LUPTON-177 (page 71), LUPTON-176 (page 70), LUPTON-035 (page 68-69, the notes cite 68)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, image, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate colour E91, joined by content E224

### logo-022 · A logo works at every size, in one colour and on dark
- **Statement:** A logo must work at every size, colour and medium, so it rests on geometric, stylised, abstract form: if it cannot work in pure black and white, it must at least work in greyscale. Its graphic form should be noticeably simpler than its lettering, and an important logo needs versions with adjusted details for small sizes (a simplified version with bold strokes still reads when severely reduced) and for light-on-dark use. Check the letterforms at small sizes too: Kirsanov's stylish Q, whose tail became hard to make out when small, turned into an O, a real risk for an invented name that is hard enough to read already, so he chose a plainer Q that reads at any size.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** KIRSANOV-436 (page 131), KIRSANOV-443 (page 134), CABARGA-055 (page 39), KIRSANOV-529 (page 163)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate images E91, joined by type E233

### logo-023 · Only flat colour inside a logo
- **Statement:** Photographic, material or sculptural textures and photographic 3D have no place in a logo itself, only flat colour and not-too-fine geometric textures. Volume can still be suggested with flat colour alone (a circle cut by bands of differing thickness reads as a sphere).
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** KIRSANOV-453 (page 137), KIRSANOV-437 (page 132), KIRSANOV-559 (page 137)

### logo-024 · A logo is recognisable and original, not a literal picture of the trade
- **Statement:** A logo's two basic demands are recognisability (from simplicity and inner logic) and originality; it stops the eye only when a small hidden meaning or paradox contrasts with its outward simplicity. A literal picture of what the company does (a book for a publisher) is in most cases the opposite of originality; most logos, the best-known among them, do well with wholly abstract forms, and a compromise is a mark built on the company's initial or abbreviation.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** KIRSANOV-438 (page 132), KIRSANOV-440 (page 133)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate images E93

### logo-026 · The name stands to the right of the sign or below it
- **Statement:** Place a logo's name to the right of or below its graphic sign, so the text reads as a caption seen after the picture. In the book's two sample layouts of one mock logo, with the name below the sign is large (about five times the cap height); with the name to the right the sign is about three times the cap height and the name sits at its vertical middle. Text in a ring around the sign looks balanced, solid and old-fashioned.

### logo-035 · Follow the owner's brand book and logo rules

### logo-038 · Give the site icons for phone and tablet home screens
- **Statement:** Beyond favicon.ico, give the site a set of icons that represent it on other devices, such as home-screen icons for phones and tablets; iOS takes its home-screen icon from the non-standard apple-touch-icon link. The book's sizes, 32 to about 228 pixels square, were those known when it was written: today Chromium needs at least a 192 and a 512 pixel icon in a web app manifest (web.dev), and MDN advises an icon of at least 1024 pixels or one that scales to that size.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** ROBBINS-381 (page 784-785, the notes cite 784)
- **Asked of the source:** which icon sizes and formats current phones, tablets and browsers ask for.
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Attributes/rel (read 2026-11-21)
- **Current source:** overturned by https://web.dev/articles/add-manifest (read 2026-11-21)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate phone E42

### logo-043 · The worst letter pair sets the spacing of a logo or headline
- **Statement:** In a logo or headline, the letter pair that cannot be set tight (the worst kerning pair) sets the spacing: either fix that pair by overlapping, narrowing or ligating letters, or open up the rest of the spacing to match its gap.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** CABARGA-080 (page 115)

### logo-044 · Logo colours: one colour, sturdy shades, within the print range
- **Statement:** Most logos use one colour, or one colour plus black or white. Avoid very dark, very light or weakly saturated shades, which do not survive the many reproductions a logo goes through, and keep within the print colour range, since a web logo will likely be printed later.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** KIRSANOV-450 (page 136)
