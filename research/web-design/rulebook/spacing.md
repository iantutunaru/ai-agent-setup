# Web design rulebook: spacing

34 entries: 1 rule, 33 practice. Built by a script from the owner's reading notes, which are not published; not edited by hand. How to read an entry: [README.md](README.md).

### spacing-001 · Plan the empty space, do not fill the page
- **Statement:** Do not fill every part of the page. Planned empty space is as important as content: it keeps the design from feeling cramped, guides the eye, creates balance and unity, helps readers get into the content and sets the product's market position. It is designed, not left over.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BEAIRD-025 (page 25), BOULTON-145 (page 108)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E01

### spacing-002 · Space added around one element gives it weight
- **Statement:** White space is passive (margins, leading, a by-product of the layout, giving breathing room and balance) or active: added on purpose around one element to emphasise or structure it, drawing the eye to it without colour or size. The more empty space surrounds an object, the more weight it carries, so even a dense business page needs pockets of space around its key elements, such as the logo and the headings.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BOULTON-144 (page 107), KIRSANOV-092 (page 38), BOULTON-136 (page 103)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E03

### spacing-003 · Dense content gets lighter with space in the small gaps
- **Statement:** Macro white space lies between major elements, micro white space between list items, between caption and image, between words and letters. Dense content that cannot be given more macro space can be made lighter and more legible by adding micro space, as in Spiekermann's redesign of the Economist, which kept the same amount of content.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BOULTON-142 (page 105-106)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E04

### spacing-004 · Separate with space, not with rules, and do not cram
- **Statement:** Avoiding useless white space must not tip into cramming: an over-dense, claustrophobic home page, such as Pathfinder's in February 1997, is to be avoided too. In the redesign example, replacing divider lines with white gaps made the page look less cluttered; the urge to insert a horizontal rule is very often a sure sign that the visual or content structure at that point has not been thought through.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-020 (page 34-35), KIRSANOV-330 (page 99)
- **Also in (not read):** NIELSEN-122
- **Conflict:** LUPTON-056 (in layout-101) holds that one dense, well-organised page can serve better than several sparse ones.
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate layout E111

### spacing-005 · Resizing an element changes the space around it
- **Statement:** The shape and size of an element determine the space around it: enlarging an element reduces that space, so a change to either changes the spacing of the layout.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** BOULTON-138 (page 103)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E05

### spacing-006 · Density of placement sets the mood
- **Statement:** Businesslike, energetic pages want tighter placement. Artistic, leisurely pages use empty space as a full element of the composition, often more of it than of the content.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** KIRSANOV-090 (page 38)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E06

### spacing-007 · Compact shapes are placed tighter than open ones
- **Statement:** Match the density of placement to the compactness of the elements: as a rule, compact, solid shapes call for tighter placement than open, holey ones.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** KIRSANOV-089 (page 38)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E07

### spacing-008 · Closeness makes the groups
- **Statement:** Objects placed close together are read as related, as one unit, and the same elements tell a different story when their distances change. Set the margins and padding between headings, paragraphs, quotes and images on purpose so that the spacing forms the groups, and group related text into clusters rather than scattering it to fill every bit of space. In one measured news homepage the gaps inside a story module are 10 px and the gap before the next module is 30 px, three times as much.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BEAIRD-039 (page 36), BOULTON-194 (page 123), GENTRY-102 (page 131), CABARGA-106 (page 125)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, image, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E09, joined by layout E57

### spacing-009 · Current design separates with space, not rule lines
- **Statement:** In current design, sections and groups are more often separated by empty space or a change of background colour than by a horizontal rule line; where dividing lines remain, they are usually part of a larger shape such as a rectangle.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** KIRSANOV-097 (page 40)
- **Also in (not read):** NIELSEN-017
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E10

### spacing-010 · A heading sits closer to the text it introduces
- **Statement:** Place each heading closer to the section it introduces than to the section above it. A heading set at equal distance from both floats and reads as a divider rather than a heading.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BEAIRD-040 (page 37), KRUG-036 (page s34)
- **Conflict:** BRINGHURST-083 (in spacing-024) allows a subhead set off by an equal blank line above and below, as well as the asymmetric setting with more space above.
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E11

### spacing-011 · A gap must still link what it separates
- **Statement:** Do not stretch space too far. In contents lists and similar structures the space should both separate and link: a gap too wide for the eye to cross unaided destroys the link, and so do dot leaders that drag the eye across the page.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BRINGHURST-071 (page 39-40)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E13

### spacing-012 · A box needs padding inside and room around it
- **Statement:** A box whose edges sit tight around its text, with no padding, is a common beginner error. The box and its content are separate, almost equal elements that in most cases need a neutral zone inside and around them.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** KIRSANOV-106 (page 42)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E14

### spacing-013 · Contrast of type can do the work of space
- **Statement:** A very narrow gap between the main text and a side column of notes (6 or 7 points beside a 33.5-pica measure) causes no confusion when the two differ clearly in size and style (14-point roman text, 12-point italic notes).
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** BRINGHURST-334 (page 200)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E15

### spacing-014 · Make elements more compact as the screen narrows
- **Statement:** On a narrow screen reduce the space elements take: a smaller tagline and intro text, smaller icons, and a form's label and button on the same line as its text field cut the scrolling down the long single column; at a narrow breakpoint cut the padding so more content fits, centre the logo and navigation links, and shrink the navigation and the main heading.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** GILLENWATER-085 (page 249-250, the notes cite 249), CEDERHOLM-087 (page 298)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate phone E27

### spacing-015 · Margins are part of the design
- **Statement:** Treat margins as part of the design: about half the character of a printed page comes from the type and the other half largely from the margins. Margins have three jobs: to lock the text block to the page and tie facing pages together through their proportions, to frame the text block in a way that suits its design, and to protect it so the reader can see it easily and hold it without covering it.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** BRINGHURST-317 (page 191-192), BRINGHURST-318 (page 192)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E16

### spacing-016 · Margins are designed space
- **Statement:** Margins and the empty space around a text block do real work: they set the content apart and hold the navigation aids (page numbers, running heads, notes, section numbers, side notes). Make the margins an object of design and organise what stands in them, so that it enlivens the page and binds it to the text block.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** BRINGHURST-319 (page 192), LUPTON-121 (page 153)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate layout E107

### spacing-017 · Give spacing in units tied to the text size
- **Statement:** Give line height, margins, padding and the gaps between elements and between text columns in ems, as the text size is, so that the spacing grows and shrinks in proportion when the reader changes the text size and the columns stay readable. With padding in ems a whole menu tab scales, not only its label.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** CEDERHOLM-016 (page 36), GILLENWATER-073 (page 234-235), CEDERHOLM-032 (page 60)
- **Asked of the source:** that spacing set in ems scales with the text when a reader enlarges the text in current browsers, and what WCAG asks of spacing when text is resized.
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C28 (read 2026-11-20)
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html (read 2026-11-20)
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Learn_web_development/Core/Styling_basics/Values_and_units (read 2026-11-20)

### spacing-018 · A few proportional intervals of space
- **Statement:** Space can be divided endlessly, but a few proportional intervals are far more useful than an unlimited choice of arbitrary amounts. Do not change the leading arbitrarily within a text; add and remove vertical space only in proportional intervals.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BRINGHURST-073 (page 40), BRINGHURST-079 (page 42)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E27

### spacing-019 · Set everything on a baseline grid
- **Statement:** Build a baseline grid from the body text's size and line spacing (for example 10 pt type on 12 pt lines, in whole numbers rather than automatic leading). One method gives every element a line height and a bottom margin equal to the body line height or a multiple of it; another gives headlines, subheads and captions line spacings that work with the grid (on a 12 pt grid, 24, 18 and 12). All elements then share one vertical rhythm. On the web this is done with the line height and margins in the style sheet. Where a layout works better off the grid, do not force it.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BEAIRD-209 (page 167), LUPTON-146 (page 198)
- **Also in (not read):** LUPTON-211, BRINGHURST-074
- **Asked of the source:** that a baseline grid can be held on a current web page with line height and margins set as multiples of the body line height.
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://web.dev/articles/baseline-in-action-container-queries (read 2026-11-20)
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://web.dev/blog/baseline-digest-may-2026 (read 2026-11-20)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E28

### spacing-020 · Line spacing that scales with each element's size
- **Statement:** Line spacing is set as a ratio of the type size, so that larger headings get proportionally taller lines; a fixed length passed down from the body text may be too tight for bigger text.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** ROBBINS-198 (page 367)
- **Asked of the source:** how line height is inherited in CSS when given as a number and when given with a unit.

### spacing-021 · Smaller text takes a fraction of the grid
- **Statement:** On a baseline grid set by the body line spacing, give smaller text such as captions a line spacing that is a fraction of the grid (incremental leading), so that it still lines up with the body every few lines and the two relate.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BOULTON-072 (page 59)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E29

### spacing-022 · More line spacing for longer lines
- **Statement:** Line spacing must exceed word spacing, so the eye travels along the line and not down, and it rises with line length. One source gives about 1.5 on average: 1.2 to 1.5 for the shorter lines of small screens, 1.4 to 1.6 for wide screens with larger text.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BOULTON-068 (page 58), ROBBINS-312 (page 605)
- **Conflict:** GENTRY-071 (in spacing-023) puts the range at 16 to 22 pt on 16 pt text, which is 1 to about 1.4.
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate type E130

### spacing-023 · A little more line spacing than seems needed
- **Statement:** Give text a little more line spacing than seems needed; one author sets 9 pt type on 13 pt lines, another prefers 16 pt type on 22 pt lines so the design breathes.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** BOULTON-071 (page 59), GENTRY-071 (page 90)
- **Conflict:** ROBBINS-312 (in spacing-022) gives a higher range, about 1.5 on average and up to 1.6.
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate type E132

### spacing-024 · An interruption takes a whole number of text lines
- **Statement:** Headings, subheads, block quotes, notes, pictures and captions break the steady rhythm of the leading, and after each one the main text must land back on its beat: the total vertical space of the interruption, with the space around it, adds up to a whole number of text lines (with 11/13 text, multiples of 13 points). A subhead can take a blank line above and below, or more space above than below, for example 8 points above and 5 below a bold subhead in 11/13 text.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BRINGHURST-082 (page 43), BRINGHURST-083 (page 43)
- **Conflict:** BEAIRD-040 and KRUG-036 (in spacing-010) say a heading at equal distance from the text above and below floats and reads as a divider; BRINGHURST-083 allows the equal setting.
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E30

### spacing-025 · Do not stretch line or paragraph spacing to fill a fixed height
- **Statement:** A rigid layout that demands a fixed text height suits no long work. Stretching the line spacing on short pages (vertical justification), or adding space between paragraphs to keep a standard height, destroys the texture of the text and should not be used; a short document may be reworked whole to fit instead.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BRINGHURST-345 (page 206)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate spacing E31

### spacing-026 · Give the text column margins on both sides
- **Statement:** A text column needs empty margins on both sides for comfortable reading, so the first step away from the browser default is to set text in a column with ample margins. The author says research shows this but names no study.
- **Kind:** rule
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** KIRSANOV-093 (page 38)
- **Study (as the book reports it):** unnamed research that, the author says, showed empty margins on both sides of a text column are necessary for comfortable reading; no study, method or figures given
- **Current source:** none found (searched 2026-11-20)

### spacing-027 · Line spacing neither too tight nor too loose
- **Statement:** Lines set too tight (16 pt type on 14 pt) are hard to tell apart, so the reader loses the place at the end of a line, and reducing the spacing risks ascenders and descenders colliding; lines set too loose (16 pt on 34 pt) make the eye jump too far, and as the spacing grows the lines stop reading as one block of text. Loose spacing makes more sense on an expressive fashion or art site than on a site that conveys information, such as news.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** GENTRY-069 (page 88), GENTRY-070 (page 89), LUPTON-073 (page 108), LUPTON-074 (page 108)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate type E131

### spacing-028 · Wide line spacing sets an unhurried tone
- **Statement:** Increased line spacing in a paragraph of more than three lines gives a free, unhurried, persuasive tone and can match a text block's height to another element; it looks best with faces whose capitals are much taller than their lowercase.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** KIRSANOV-205 (page 68-69)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate type E134

### spacing-029 · Stacked lines of mixed sizes are spaced by eye
- **Statement:** When lines of mixed sizes or styles are stacked, automatic line spacing gives uneven gaps; adjust the spacing by hand until the lines look evenly spaced. When lines of capitals and lines of lowercase alternate, for example, the gaps between the lines look uneven, because capitals have no descenders: shift individual baselines so that the space looks even rather than measures even.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** LUPTON-075 (page 108), LUPTON-026 (page 52)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate type E135, joined by spacing E37

### spacing-030 · A button sits with the text it follows
- **Statement:** Place a call to action, such as a "read more" button, right with the text it follows; a button set apart from its paragraph looks unrelated. Keep a headline and its subheadline close together, and space headline, subheadline and button evenly as one group so they are read as belonging together when the page is scanned.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** GENTRY-106 (page 135-136), GENTRY-105 (page 134)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate layout E58

### spacing-031 · Unequal margins give a block of text direction
- **Statement:** A square text block centred on a square page with equal margins is unlikely to invite reading, because reading needs orientation. Break the uniformity: make one space narrow so another can be wide, and empty one so another can be filled, to give direction, liveliness and balance.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** BRINGHURST-315 (page 190)
- **Also in (not read):** BRINGHURST-316, LUPTON-134
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate layout E89

### spacing-032 · A modular scale in place of a grid
- **Statement:** A modular scale does the same job as a grid but more flexibly: it is a preset series of harmonic proportions, a ruler whose units are unequal, such as the traditional type-size series or a Fibonacci series. A scale built on two ratios (for example 1:1.618 and 1:2) gives more flexible and interesting results than one built on a single ratio.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** BRINGHURST-326 (page 194), BRINGHURST-329 (page 197)
- **Also in (not read):** BRINGHURST-327, BRINGHURST-328
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, image, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate layout E95

### spacing-033 · Grid rows are a whole number of text lines
- **Statement:** Make the rows of a modular grid a whole number of text lines: count the lines in a full column and divide by a number that goes evenly (42 lines make 7 modules of 6 lines), letting the margins absorb any remainder.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** LUPTON-147 (page 198)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate layout E100

### spacing-034 · Generous white space reads as upscale, cramped space as down-market
- **Statement:** Generous white space positions a brand as upscale and sophisticated (luxury goods, cosmetics), while cramped layouts with bold type and little space read as down-market. In the author's example the same content and photographs, laid out with less or more white space, sit at opposite ends of the brand spectrum, though he notes that a brand's position takes more than white space.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** BOULTON-143 (page 106)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate trust E62
