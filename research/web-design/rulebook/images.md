### images-001 · Pictures are remembered better than words
- **Statement:** Pictures are remembered remarkably well, and by the book's sources better than what is heard or read: in one study people shown 2,560 photographs for 10 seconds each recognised over 90 percent of them when tested up to three days later (Standing, Conezio and Haber, 1970). The book adds that pictures are remembered a year later with 63 percent accuracy, against about 10 percent of what people hear or read without pictures. Present information as pictures where possible: the book calls it the most efficient way to present information.
- **Kind:** rule
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** NEURO-087 (page 130), NEURO-088 (page 131)
- **Study (as the book reports it):** research from the 1960s to the present summarised by Brady, 2008, and research from the 1960s reviewed by John J. Medina in Brain Rules; sizes not given
- **Needs source:** a study comparing memory for pictures with memory for heard or read words, and the 63 percent after a year
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03337426 (read 2026-11-20)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate images E01

### images-002 · Images draw visitors in, and the wrong ones harm the site
- **Statement:** Photographs and illustrations often draw passing visitors into the content, but the wrong images, or the right ones poorly presented, harm a site's appeal, and each viewer reads an image through their own background and experience. Poor graphics can drive a reader to switch images off: one author keeps image loading off to escape the flood of third-rate graphics, even on sites with good content.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BEAIRD-217 (page 172), KIRSANOV-402 (page 118)

### images-006 · Do not illustrate the subject too literally
- **Statement:** Do not always put literal relevance first when choosing images: being too literal leads straight to clichés, and an unexpected but interesting image or concept can serve the site better. A picture placed with words should do more than illustrate them: it should help understanding and add comment, humour or an invitation to read or buy.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look

### images-016 · An image of text takes exactly that text as its alt
- **Statement:** When an image shows a heading, a button label or other text, its alt text is exactly that text, word for word and nothing more; the lettering's decorative effects are not described. Screen readers, text browsers and robots then get the text.

### images-017 · People expect to click the parts of a diagram or annotated picture
- **Statement:** Simple diagrams such as a timeline help scanning and show the structure of an argument. A small thumbnail on the main page can link to a large annotated picture, as on the author's Starfire site (1995); users keep trying to click the annotations on such a picture, hoping for more information about parts of the image.

### images-018 · Make a thumbnail by cropping and scaling together
- **Statement:** To make a small version of a picture, combine cropping and scaling: scaling alone makes it illegible, cropping alone loses context. For a thumbnail at 10% of the area, crop to about 32% and scale that to 32%, keeping the crop centred on the action.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-226 (page 170, 172-173, the notes cite 170)

### images-020 · Reuse the same images across the site
- **Statement:** Reuse the same images: repeats load faster from the cache, and a small visual vocabulary of buttons, arrows and icons used over and over ties the site together. A large image reused across pages keeps its meaning because users recognise it; two different concepts probably call for two different images, even at the cost of loading time.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-053 (page 62-63, the notes cite 63)
- **Asked of the source:** that a repeated image file is still served from the browser's cache on later pages.
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Guides/Caching (read 2026-11-20)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate images E33

### images-021 · Use SVG for drawings, and keep large reused ones out of the HTML
- **Statement:** For certain kinds of images, an SVG file stores only drawing instructions, so it is usually smaller than the same picture as a raster image and loads faster; being text, it can also be compressed with Gzip or Brotli like HTML. SVG written into the page bloats the HTML, makes the images hard to maintain and is not cached apart from the page, so avoid that for large images reused across many pages.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** ROBBINS-085 (page 190-191), ROBBINS-089 (page 194)
- **Asked of the source:** that SVG files are compressed in transit by current servers, and that SVG placed inline is not cached separately from its page (MDN, web.dev).
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Learn_web_development/Core/Structuring_content/Including_vector_graphics_in_HTML (read 2026-11-20)
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://web.dev/articles/reduce-network-payloads-using-text-compression (read 2026-11-20)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate speed E50

### images-022 · Avoid text inside images
- **Statement:** Avoid putting text inside images (headings, titles, buttons, menus): people cannot change the size, colours or font of text in an image, so present it as real text in a web font wherever that gives the same look (WCAG 1.4.5, which exempts logotypes). Real text can be resized, selected, searched, indexed, reformatted and restyled for other media, and an editor changes the words without remaking an image for every label and state; text in images also slows downloads and makes translation of the interface harder. Where it cannot be avoided, make the text fairly large so it stays legible, give small screens alternative versions with larger text, and give high-density screens files with enough pixels, which they need to look sharp.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-029 (page 41), ROBBINS-324 (page 607), GOLDSTEIN-031 (page 241), GILLENWATER-039 (page 146), GILLENWATER-052 (page 161), GILLENWATER-014 (page 37), LUPTON-054 (page 98), CEDERHOLM-023 (page 46)
- **Also in (not read):** KIRSANOV-380, NIELSEN-056, NIELSEN-583, BEAIRD-177
- **Asked of the source:** current guidance on images of text (WCAG) and on how images scale on high-density screens.
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/images-of-text.html (read 2026-11-20)
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Guides/Responsive_images (read 2026-11-20)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate images E35, joined by type E213, joined by content E37, joined by content E38

### images-025 · Decoration costs loading time
- **Statement:** Complex textures, 3D effects included, inevitably swell image files and lengthen page loading. Sites meant for frequent repeat visits keep decorative elements to a minimum, in visibility and in file size, so that readers do not waste time downloading graphics and nothing distracts from the content they came for.

### images-026 · Choose the visual style from the brand, the purpose and the concept
- **Statement:** Do not pick a site's visual style because it is in fashion. Choose it from the essence of the brand, then the purpose and concept of the site, and only then from what you like. The brand limits the style (a brand that only uses photography makes illustration a poor fit; for a large client such as McDonald's an edgy collage is probably out) and the style should complement the purpose (a highly functional shop does not work in a loose collage).

### images-031 · People and objects in a picture face into the content
- **Statement:** The direction a pictured person or object faces steers the eye: facing into the content leads the eye into the page, facing straight ahead feels static, and facing off the edge draws the eye off the page. - **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check

### images-032 · Place the subject off-centre, with room on the side it faces
- **Statement:** Place a photo's focal point off-centre, with perspective lines leading to it, for a more interesting and balanced composition, and leave empty space ("looking room") on the side the subject faces or moves toward, which gives the image movement. A subject framed with equal space on both sides is static, and space behind the subject makes the eye go back and forth and the composition uncomfortable.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** BEAIRD-241 (page 188), BOULTON-130 (page 97-99)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate images E44

### images-033 · A moving subject needs free space and a line to follow
- **Statement:** A picture of something that moves adds dynamics, but must suit the work's subject and style and be supported by the elements around it. The moving element needs free space: more space behind it reads as slowing down, more in front as speeding up, just started, and space on both sides as most active. Its direction line must be coordinated with the page's other lines, usually by parallel linear elements, and is a natural way to bring a diagonal into a page.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** KIRSANOV-261 (page 81), KIRSANOV-262 (page 81)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate images E45

### images-040 · Images are the heaviest part of a home page, so keep every file as small as possible
- **Statement:** Images are still the largest single kind of resource on home pages, though no longer most of their bytes: the median mobile home page of 2025 carried 911 KB of images in 2.6 MB, and inner pages 354 KB (HTTP Archive Web Almanac); the author's 60 to 70 percent names no study. Images bring a page to life, yet they try users' patience on slow connections and use up mobile users' paid data, so plan the images around page speed and make each file as small as possible.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** ROBBINS-383 (page 788), ROBBINS-421 (page 812)
- **Asked of the source:** the share of a page's bytes that images take today (HTTP Archive, web.dev); the 60 to 70 percent dates from 2018 to 2021.
- **Current source:** overturned by https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/page-weight (read 2026-11-20)
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2024/page-weight (read 2026-11-20)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate speed E41

### images-042 · No sharp photograph or busy texture behind text
- **Statement:** A sharp, unblurred photograph used as a background behind text is a serious and common mistake; a blur turns an image into background. A background texture with small sharp details must not sit behind small text, since it makes reading very hard, and is best not placed next to text either.

### images-067 · Every image has an alt text, a helpful one or an empty one
- **Statement:** Every image carries an alt text, which is what is shown or read when the image is not available. An image without one leaves a user who cannot see it with no idea what they missed, while good alternative texts make the page usable with images off or through a speech synthesiser, and tell a blind user what information the page holds. Give every image a helpful description, except an image that adds nothing to the page's content (a spacer, a decorative flourish, the scaffolding of the visual layout): it gets an empty alt text, with nothing at all between the quotes, rather than none at all, so that a screen reader skips it and readers of the text version are not bothered by it. The empty value is not the default: the author has to set it on every such image. Without one, one book says most screen readers announced an unidentified image, since they could not tell how important it was; W3C now says some read out the image's file name. ### images-098 · Where cutting colours stops paying
