### testing-001 · Opinion and satisfaction surveys do not show what people do
- **Statement:** Opinion surveys are usually a weak method for web design, because what people say and what they do differ; a questionnaire can be trusted when it asks only for opinions. Satisfaction ratings can mislead: three in four members of a scheme called their experience positive, yet a year in 73% said it was too complex and 60% wanted the number of plans limited, and the authors doubt that people had a sound basis for judging whether they had chosen well.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** THALER-175 (page 149-150), NIELSEN-609 (page 476)
- **Study (as the book reports it):** THALER-175: surveys of Medicare members, November 2005 to November 2007, one of 1,800 people; survey of doctors and pharmacists.
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E04

### testing-002 · A vote can settle the visual style, not the usable design
- **Statement:** A popularity vote among users rarely picks the most usable design, but the overall visual style matters little to usability, so a vote can settle it. Sun put parallel design sketches of its intranet to a staff vote, so that staff would feel part of the design, kept the chosen style, and then changed single elements after usability tests to make text more readable and icons easier to understand.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-436 (page 325), NIELSEN-437 (page 330)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E05

### testing-003 · A test shows the problem, the fix still has to be thought up
- **Statement:** Data alone does not finish a design. After a test shows a problem (users cannot find the search button), finding the right fix still takes ideas; the best results come from combining the systematic method with close watching of how users react.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-004 (page 19-20, the notes cite 20)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E08

### testing-004 · A test with one person beats no test
- **Statement:** A test with one user beats no test by 100 percent, and even a poor test with the wrong user shows important improvements. Keep tests simple enough that they actually happen: a few strangers in a coffee shop, offered a coffee and a muffin for ten minutes, are better than no testing at all.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** KRUG-101 (page s78), WALTER-076 (page 85)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E11

### testing-005 · Useful testing needs no lab, no large group and no long report
- **Statement:** A lab and many participants are not needed for useful results. Do-it-yourself testing takes a room with a desk, a computer and two chairs, three loosely recruited participants and one morning a month, with the team watching from another room and a one to two page email of the decisions as the report; it aims at the most serious problems, which are hard to miss, not at a ranked list of hundreds. The book gives the cost as a few hundred dollars a round, against $5,000 to $10,000 for a traditional round; NN/g today says a simple study can run in a conference room for a few hundred dollars of incentives, and puts a remote moderated study of five participants at about $415 to $1,680 plus 32 to 48 researcher hours (2020).
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** KRUG-104 (page s79), KRUG-217 (page s79), KRUG-220 (page s94)
- **Study (as the book reports it):** KRUG-104: Jakob Nielsen, "Usability Engineering at a Discount", 1989.
- **Asked of the source:** what a round of usability testing costs today, hired out and done in-house (Nielsen Norman Group); the dollar figures are from 1989 and 2013.
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-testing-101/ (read 2026-11-21)
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://www.nngroup.com/articles/remote-usability-testing-costs/ (read 2026-11-21)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, image, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E12

### testing-006 · Test on a fixed day, not at a milestone
- **Statement:** Test on a fixed schedule: one morning a month with three users, a debrief over lunch and a decision on what to fix before the next round (or two users every two weeks in an Agile team). A set day, unlike a milestone, keeps testing from slipping and makes it easy for the team to come and watch.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** KRUG-105 (page s80)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E13

### testing-007 · Three participants a round, and more rounds before more people
- **Statement:** Three participants per round is ideal for do-it-yourself testing. It is qualitative work meant to find and fix problems, not to prove anything, and three users meet many of the most serious problems for the tasks tested. More rounds matter more than more users per round.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** KRUG-106 (page s81)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E14

### testing-008 · Recruit typical users and give them typical tasks
- **Statement:** For any site the core rule of a user test is the same: recruit typical users and have them do typical tasks. Research done on whoever is at hand, such as asking a relative to try the site, may be inaccurate or unrepresentative. For an intranet, recruit staff through department heads and notices on the intranet or a notice board; a quick poll of randomly chosen staff on a page or an icon does for a simple test but not for a serious study, since the group tends to be less varied.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-471 (page 349), BOULTON-032 (page 34-35)
- **Conflict:** KRUG-108 (in testing-086) says to recruit loosely and not to let a narrow profile reduce how much is tested.
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E15

### testing-009 · Include new staff when testing an intranet's home page and navigation
- **Statement:** Even though intranet tools are aimed mainly at experienced staff, include some newcomers when testing the intranet's home page and navigation. New staff rely on the intranet to learn their working environment and must not be confused by new and unfamiliar concepts.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-472 (page 350)

### testing-010 · Pay participants a little above the going rate
- **Statement:** Pay test participants a little above the going rate: it shows that their time is valued and makes them likelier to show up, and they also spend time travelling. The book's figures are $50 to $100 an hour for ordinary users and several hundred for busy professionals; NN/g recommends fairly generous incentives against no-shows, higher for busy, rare or affluent people, and gives $80 to $250 a participant for a remote moderated session (2020).
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** KRUG-110 (page s83)
- **Asked of the source:** what test participants are paid today (Nielsen Norman Group); the amounts are from 2013.

### testing-011 · The shape of a one-hour session
- **Statement:** A one-hour test runs roughly: welcome (4 minutes), background questions (2), a tour of the home page in which the participant says what they make of it without clicking (3), the tasks (35), probing questions (5), wrap-up and payment (5). Read the script lines as written.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** KRUG-117 (page s89-s90, the notes cite s89)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E23

### testing-012 · Do not lead or help the participant during the tasks
- **Statement:** During tasks ask no leading questions and give no help unless the participant is hopelessly stuck or extremely frustrated. Prompt with "What are you thinking?"; when asked for help, ask what they would do if you were not there. Keep questions about what happened for the probing time at the end. In intranet tests, turn down requests for help early on, explaining that the aim is to see what staff do with no one to ask; near the end you can step out of the observer role and help, as thanks and to learn what they needed and why they could not do it alone.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** KRUG-118 (page s89), NIELSEN-479 (page 370)
- **Also in (not read):** WALTER-074
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E25

### testing-013 · Get the team and the managers to watch
- **Statement:** Get as many people as possible (team, stakeholders, managers, executives) to watch test sessions; after each session every observer writes down the three most serious problems seen. Seeing users struggle convinces observers that users are not all like them. In one hospital project, a video shot from a patient's point of view made the problem real to the staff and gave them the will to fix it, where a problem list had not.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** KRUG-113 (page s86), HEATH-104 (page s87)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E26

### testing-014 · Watch faces at the moments meant to delight
- **Statement:** In simple tests with about three users, with the organisation's leaders invited to watch, record the sessions and look at the users' faces at key moments, to see whether a moment meant to delight made them smile or went unnoticed.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** WALTER-075 (page 85)
- **Conflict:** KRUG-148 (in phone-042) says to skip a face camera in phone tests, because the voice shows feelings well enough.

### testing-015 · One participant's liking for the looks proves nothing
- **Statement:** Do not make much of individual reactions to a site's looks in tests. The next participant is as likely to dislike the colour the last one liked.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** KRUG-120 (page s90)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E31

### testing-016 · What remote testing gains and loses
- **Statement:** Remote testing over screen sharing widens recruiting to almost anyone and makes it easier for busy people to take part; unmoderated remote services return a recorded think-aloud session quickly and cheaply, at the cost of no live interaction. The drawbacks the older book lists: you cannot see what the user does and must follow their sparse descriptions, users may have to install unfamiliar tools, and time zones force odd working hours. For tests by international phone call, pay for the calls and say so when recruiting.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** KRUG-127 (page s93), NIELSEN-567 (page 425)
- **Asked of the source:** how moderated and unmoderated remote usability tests are run today and what each loses (Nielsen Norman Group); the books name a service of 2013 and the telephony of about 1999.
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://www.nngroup.com/articles/remote-usability-tests/ (read 2026-11-21)
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://www.nngroup.com/articles/remote-usability-testing-costs/ (read 2026-11-21)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E46

### testing-017 · Studies that need no experimenter can be run by the users themselves
- **Statement:** Any study that needs no experimenter can be run by users from written instructions: find given information and report whether and how fast they found it, comment freely on draft pages, or rate their looks and navigation on a seven-point scale. Ask for the report in the same medium as the instructions.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-571 (page 428)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E48

### testing-018 · Invite chosen users to a self-run test, do not let them pick themselves
- **Statement:** For a self-run test in other countries, send a personal invitation to users chosen at random in the countries studied. A link on the home page or a notice to a mailing list makes participants select themselves and skews the results towards more experienced, active users.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-573 (page 429)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E49

### testing-019 · Ask participants their country, do not guess it from the address
- **Statement:** In a study run across countries, ask participants their country, or their region from a list, even when the questions are kept few. Guessing the country from the domain name misleads, since many people outside the USA use .com addresses and a visitor may be abroad: ask for the country when figures per country matter, and otherwise read domain-based results with caution.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-574 (page 429-431)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E50

### testing-020 · Translate self-test forms, and the scale labels with care
- **Statement:** Translate the instructions and forms of a self-run test into the users' native language. Fixed rating scales without free-text questions avoid translating answers back, but translate the scale labels with care and avoid slang, since cultures answer differently: in many Asian countries people refrain from giving too many negative ratings, and in some countries people dislike overstatement and will not "fully agree" with a gushing statement.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check, text
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-572 (page 428-429)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E52

### testing-021 · Watch people where they really use the product
- **Statement:** Field studies, watching people at their real work, matter alongside lab tests for any site; on an intranet even more, since many of its parts support specialists: easy-to-understand icons are worthless if the page layout does not fit how the task is actually done. Watching people at work often reveals unexpected actions that lab tests had not foreseen. In one company's case, visiting customers at home, where they use the product, gave a manager who knew their demographics by heart a picture the data had not.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-474 (page 351), HEATH-060 (page s62), NIELSEN-480 (page 370)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E53

### testing-022 · In an intranet field study the observer stays silent
- **Statement:** In an intranet field study the observer stays mostly silent and unobtrusive, so that staff work as usual. Note puzzling actions and see whether a repeat explains them, ask only when strictly necessary, and keep the rest for a talk afterwards.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-477 (page 369)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E54

### testing-023 · Video pays when users speak another language or the team cannot attend
- **Statement:** Video earns its cost in international tests when users speak a foreign language, since comments need replaying or an interpreter who writes them down, and when most of the team cannot attend: showing them video is far more useful than a written report, and watching people use their work gives them a positive experience. When paying a foreign consultant's lab, also pay for an edited tape of the moments that show the key problems, with the users' comments translated as subtitles.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-569 (page 426), NIELSEN-570 (page 427)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E56

### testing-024 · Five ways to test in another country
- **Statement:** There are five ways to test international use: travel there yourself, test remotely, hire a local usability consultant, have your local office staff run the test, or let users test by themselves. Very large companies can also set up usability teams in their main markets.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-561 (page 422)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E59

### testing-025 · When you travel to test, test in the users' surroundings and check the equipment first
- **Statement:** Testing in person abroad, ideally in the users' own surroundings, reveals many small but important details that no report conveys; photos of the sessions on the team's wall remind everyone of the differences between countries. Spend the first couple of days at the local office checking all equipment and software, because running a session (watching the user, the interface, the tasks, the notes and the equipment at once, perhaps in a foreign language) leaves no room for problems.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-562 (page 422-423), NIELSEN-564 (page 423)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E60

### testing-026 · Test pages on trips made for other reasons
- **Statement:** Test web pages cheaply during trips made for other reasons, since a page runs on any computer. In the author's test with local users in Sweden people could not tell the "Information" and "Documentation" buttons apart, which shows that tests abroad can also expose problems that users at home have.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-563 (page 423)

### testing-027 · Allow users from other countries half as much time again
- **Statement:** Users from other countries often take longer. In the author's tests of American online shops, European users needed about 45 minutes for tasks most Americans did in 30, since they were translating the interface and working out American business concepts; plan for 50% more time per task.
- **Kind:** rule
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-558 (page 420)
- **Study (as the book reports it):** NIELSEN-558: the author's tests of American e-commerce sites with American and European users.

### testing-028 · Settle rewards and the user profile for each country

### testing-029 · Research the users before trusting what the team believes about them
- **Statement:** User research replaces assumptions and stereotypes with what readers actually want. A team's informal, accumulated knowledge of its users is valuable, but without formal research it misses needs nobody states, and a project that meets every internal requirement can still fail if users do not read the content. Interview users first, then build personas from the interviews; whether the personas are a formal document or a whiteboard sketch matters less than making them.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, text
- **Sources:** KISSANE-063 (page 37-38, the notes cite 38), KISSANE-064 (page 38)

### testing-030 · Ask three to six people from the audience open questions
- **Statement:** Talk to three to six people from the target audience and ask open questions: their first reaction, how the site makes them feel, who the site would be if it were a person, whether they would recommend it, and which parts matter most to them.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** WALTER-073 (page 84)

### testing-031 · Look at competitors' sites in an ordered way
- **Statement:** A competitive review can be a light audit: look at competitors' sites systematically, describe what you find in general terms, note features that seem excellent or terrible, and end with a list of what works and what to avoid; for some projects, such as a non-profit's, look beyond the same sector to organisations with similar tasks or audiences. For an intranet, where other organisations' intranets cannot be tested, ask newly hired staff what they liked and disliked about the intranet at their previous job, without pressing for confidential details.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** KISSANE-071 (page 40), NIELSEN-473 (page 350)

### testing-034 · A count of visitors is a misleading measure
- **Statement:** Counting unique visitors misleads, since a big advertising campaign can bring crowds who glance at the home page and never return; one book holds that the only real measure of success is steady repeat traffic from loyal users. Visitors' actions can be tracked and analysed, so content work needs a serious plan for measuring what works and what does not, beyond counting visitors or clicks.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-607 (page 475-476), KISSANE-047 (page 25)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E73

### testing-035 · Test a change against the original on one key number
- **Statement:** Pick one key metric, such as time on site or the number of sales enquiries, change the interface (in the book, by emotional design), and test the changed version against the original. An improved number is the strongest case for doing more of the same.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** WALTER-091 (page 101)

### testing-036 · A pattern seen after the fact in a few results is weak evidence
- **Statement:** Whether data look patterned can depend on how they are cut: the same map of hits divided into squares suggests clustering, divided into diagonal sectors it does not, so a pattern found only after looking at the data, with one chosen grouping, is weak evidence. The book adds that confident talk of a streak predicted little: in the 2003 NBA three-point contest, players whom commentators had just called "hot" went on to score at 55.2%, barely above the contest's overall 53.9%. Later reanalyses of the same contest, correcting a statistical bias, found real hot-hand shooting (Miller and Sanjurjo) or found it for consecutive shots from the same spot (Lantis and Nesson, 2022), so the "hot hand is a myth" reading no longer holds.
- **Kind:** rule
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** THALER-028 (page 37-38), THALER-029 (page 38-39)
- **Study (as the book reports it):** THALER-028: Gilovich 1991, reanalysis of the London V-1 map; THALER-029: Jay Koehler and Caryn Conley, NBA All-Star three-point contest, 2003.
- **Needs source:** the V-1 map reanalysis (Clarke 1946, as Gilovich reports it) on a readable page; only a PDF found
- **Current source:** overturned by https://ideas.repec.org/p/igi/igierp/548.html (read 2026-11-21)
- **Current source:** overturned by https://www.nber.org/digest/202202/exploring-hot-hand-basketball (read 2026-11-21)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E76

### testing-039 · The same page can look different in each browser
- **Statement:** The designer cannot know which browser and version a visitor uses. In the books' day the same page rendered noticeably differently across browsers and versions (alignment of graphics, spacing, line breaks, line length, tolerance of small HTML errors); browsers follow the standards much better today, but differences and bugs still creep through, and a site need not look the same everywhere as long as its core works. Pages cannot be tested on every browser if the design tries to fix exactly how a page looks; following the standards is the only way for a page to survive, and serving a separate style sheet per detected browser is a workaround that should have died with Netscape 4.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-045 (page 53-54), BOULTON-008 (page 14-15)
- **Also in (not read):** KIRSANOV-005
- **Asked of the source:** how far current browsers still render the same page differently and which browsers a check should cover today (MDN on cross-browser testing); the books name the browsers of 1999 and 2008.
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Learn_web_development/Extensions/Testing/Introduction (read 2026-11-21)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E96

### testing-041 · A browser may show less, but must not show a broken page
- **Statement:** Every browser must get the same content. An enhancement that some browsers cannot show is acceptable as long as it does not make the site worse in them, but anything that looks broken or unfinished in an older browser must be fixed. A fallback is required when a missing effect breaks the page and not just changes it: a semitransparent background a browser cannot read becomes fully transparent and the text over it may become unreadable, so test in several browsers to see which case applies.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** GILLENWATER-006 (page 33), GILLENWATER-025 (page 50-51, the notes cite 50)
- **Also in (not read):** NIELSEN-037
- **Asked of the source:** that a browser which cannot read a semitransparent background colour shows no background at all, and whether any browser in use today still cannot (MDN).
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Learn_web_development/Extensions/Testing/HTML_and_CSS (read 2026-11-21)
- **Read:** 2026-11-21, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate testing E99

### testing-042 · Decide support for old browsers from the site's own visitor statistics
- **Statement:** Agree at the start which browsers get the full design and which the basic one. Decide whether a fallback that degrades some users' experience is acceptable, and whether to support browsers that lack a feature, from the browser statistics of your own site or of the planned audience, and keep watching those statistics as they change. On a site with hundreds of thousands of visitors even 1% is a large number of people leaving dissatisfied.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** CEDERHOLM-019 (page 39), ROBBINS-290 (page 596), GILLENWATER-031 (page 61)
- **Also in (not read):** ROBBINS-092
- **Asked of the source:** which browsers and versions still need separate support today and where current usage figures are published (MDN, web.dev Baseline); the book's example is Internet Explorer 8.
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Learn_web_development/Extensions/Testing/Testing_strategies (read 2026-11-21)

### testing-084 · Test often and fix first what confuses everyone

### testing-092 · Debrief at once and fix the most serious problems first
- **Statement:** Half a day of testing finds more problems than can be fixed in a month, so always fix the most serious first. ### testing-099 · Have people in other countries review an early design
- **Statement:** In an international inspection people from different countries, ideally usability specialists there, review the site and say what would cause problems in their country.
