### trust-001 · A page is judged within milliseconds
- **Statement:** People form a general impression of a page (its looks, the amount of content, its regions, what attracts them) within milliseconds, and these snap judgments are close to the views they hold after spending time on the page.
- **Kind:** rule
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** KRUG-082 (page s63)
- **Study (as the book reports it):** KRUG-082: experiment titled "Attention Web Designers: You Have 50 Milliseconds to Make a Good First Impression!"; no authors or numbers given on the page.
- **Needs source:** whether the first glance also judges the amount of content, the regions and what draws the eye (the study as NN/g reports it covers visual appeal only)
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://www.nngroup.com/articles/first-impressions-human-automaticity/ (read 2026-11-20)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate trust E01

### trust-002 · The look has to show a professional operation
- **Statement:** Users cannot easily tell whether a website is reliable, because anyone can publish one, so a main goal of the design is to show the site as a professional operation. The look is the first thing they see and well-chosen graphics are the best way to show credibility; amateur clutter (heavy background images, animated "mail me" text, spinning envelopes) undermines trust in the site.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-126 (page 114-115, the notes cite 115), NIELSEN-127 (page 115-116, the notes cite 115)
- **Also in (not read):** BEAIRD-003, KIRSANOV-229
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate trust E02

### trust-003 · A sloppy look costs goodwill, a plain one does not drive people away
- **Statement:** A sloppy, disorganised or amateurish-looking site loses goodwill, but almost nobody leaves a site just because it is not beautiful. In a test with users, ignore comments about colour unless most participants react with strong disgust.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** KRUG-155 (page s116)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate trust E03

### trust-004 · Visible polish stands for care behind the scenes
- **Statement:** Trust comes from instinct rather than reasoned judgement, and visual design affects emotions very powerfully, perhaps more than any other stimulus; for Mint, a free service that asked people for their bank credentials, trust was the main barrier to overcome. Visible care and polish make users assume the same care is taken with what they cannot see, such as security and privacy; the design conveys this better than repeating security claims in the copy.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** WALTER-066 (page 79), WALTER-064 (page 77)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, passage, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate trust E04

### trust-005 · Every difficulty lowers the visitor's goodwill
- **Statement:** Visitors arrive with a reservoir of goodwill that each problem lowers. It varies by person and situation (hurry, a bad earlier experience), can be refilled by showing care, and can be emptied by a single mistake such as a registration form with many fields; when it runs out they leave, return less or speak badly of the organisation. Making people puzzle over simple things shows the builders did not care, which weakens trust in the site and in the organisation behind it.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** KRUG-150 (page s115), KRUG-008 (page s19)
- **Also in (not read):** KRUG-056, NIELSEN-009
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate trust E07

### trust-006 · Good experiences outweigh later faults
- **Statement:** Something will go wrong sooner or later, and goodwill earned through good experiences acts as insurance: memories of inconveniences fade while the good ones stay (the rosy effect), so the flaws of a site do not by themselves drive users away as long as the good outweighs the bad.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** WALTER-077 (page 86-87), WALTER-080 (page 90)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate trust E09

### trust-007 · A friendly interface builds trust and a better-tempered contact
- **Statement:** Friendly design and language are not window dressing: repeated positive moments build a positive memory of the product, which raises the chance that users keep using it and trust it. At MailChimp a cheerful interface also set the tone of customers' own contact: support requests arrived in a better mood, which made problems easier to solve and support conversations shorter.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, text
- **Sources:** WALTER-010 (page 21), WALTER-058 (page 72-73, the notes cite 73)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate trust E10

### trust-009 · Do not hide the phone number, the prices or the extra costs
- **Statement:** Do not hide support phone numbers, shipping rates or prices, and be upfront about what you would rather not say (shipping costs, extra fees, outages). A visible phone number, even on every page, keeps people searching the site longer; prices met late in a process feel like a sales trick; candour may lose points on the price but gains more.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check, text
- **Sources:** KRUG-151 (page s116), KRUG-158 (page s117)
- **Also in (not read):** KISSANE-042
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate trust E14

### trust-010 · Give one complete price that can be compared
- **Statement:** Pricing split into many separate fees is opaque; the authors propose setting every price out in a plain, complete, comparable table instead of long fine print, which would help people compare and choose. In more than 7,000 FHA-insured mortgages the features that hamper comparison (points, seller fees) added cost, borrowers from areas where most adults had only finished high school paid about 1,160 dollars more and broker loans cost about 600 dollars more; borrowers who paid no upfront origination fee got by far the best loans, probably because they then had only the interest rate to compare.
- **Kind:** rule
- **Use:** look, check, text
- **Sources:** THALER-154 (page 122-123), THALER-158 (page 126), THALER-113 (page 90-91)
- **Study (as the book reports it):** THALER-154: Susan Woodward, more than 7,000 FHA-insured loans; THALER-158: Susan Woodward's mortgage study.
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://huduser.gov/portal/node/381 (read 2026-11-20)
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.102.7.3249 (read 2026-11-20)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate trust E15

### trust-011 · Buying online removes unfair price differences
- **Statement:** Buying online can remove unfair price differences: in a study of US new-car sales (NBER, 2001), African-American and Hispanic buyers paid about 2% more in the showroom but nearly the same as white buyers when they bought through an online referral service, whatever their income.
- **Kind:** rule
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** THALER-159 (page 127)

### trust-013 · Published information works even when few people read it
- **Statement:** Publishing data alone can change behaviour: the US Toxics Release Inventory only required companies to disclose emissions, yet releases fell sharply, because media and activists ranked the worst offenders and no company wanted to be on that list. Mandatory labels work even if most people ignore them: it is enough that some people read them.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, text
- **Sources:** THALER-198 (page 173), THALER-241 (page 225)
- **Also in (not read):** THALER-205

### trust-015 · Do not block the prices of other regions

### trust-016 · No fake controls drawn into an advert
- **Statement:** Fake interface controls drawn into an advertisement (false buttons, input fields, scroll bars) exploit the reflex to click and are a trick below the belt; the audience soon became immune to the painted scroll bars. Real working controls in an ad (a genuine drop-down, checkboxes, a text field) feel far less deceptive, even when every choice leads to the same page.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** KIRSANOV-476 (page 143), KIRSANOV-477 (page 144)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate trust E24

### trust-018 · Do not show another site's content inside your own frame
- **Statement:** Showing other sites' content inside your own frame misleads users about whose content they are reading, since the address bar and your navigation suggest it is yours; the author regards it as copyright infringement, noting he is not a lawyer.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** check
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-125 (page 112)
- **Asked of the source:** whether a page can still show another site's content in a frame under its own address, and what current guidance says about doing so.

### trust-019 · After the facts, a gift or a contest turns the stress around
- **Statement:** Once the facts of an outage or mistake are out and tempers have cooled, redirect attention with a free gift, or with a contest where a gift for everyone is too costly (Flickr's colouring contest during an outage); it rekindles goodwill and gives users something else to focus on while the problem is fixed.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** text
- **Sources:** WALTER-079 (page 88-89, the notes cite 89)

### trust-024 · Say who the reviewer is and let them tell a story
- **Statement:** Reviews influence in this order: a known person telling a story; an unknown but imaginable person (name, company, persona) telling a story; an unimaginable person telling a story; least of all a bare rating from an unknown person. Give more about the reviewer than a name and a date.

### trust-025 · A claim is judged by the standing of its source
- **Statement:** People judge a claim by the status of whoever makes it and where it comes from: a true discovery by a junior doctor in a provincial city was disbelieved for years. ### trust-027 · Let people check the claim for themselves
- **Statement:** A claim the audience can check for themselves (a "testable credential") is a strong source of belief, and letting sceptical people see or try something convinces them where telling does not. After Wendy's "Where's the beef?" ads challenged people to compare burger sizes, the number of customers who believed its burger was bigger rose 47 percent in two months (Wendy's own polls) and revenue rose 31 percent in the first full year.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, text
- **Sources:** HEATH-075 (page s72), HEATH-145 (page s115)
- **Also in (not read):** HEATH-151
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate trust E49

### trust-031 · A brand built on feeling holds where one built on price does not
- **Statement:** Branding (how a company talks, looks and makes people feel) matters because most people decide on emotion, not on specifications. A brand built only on price earns no loyalty once competitors match the price, while one built on delight can charge more.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, text
- **Sources:** GENTRY-016 (page 28)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate trust E53

### trust-032 · Give the brand a distinct character, even if some dislike it
- **Statement:** A brand needs distinct attributes to be remembered: one without them is a commodity people will not pay much for, one that tries to please everyone loses the respect of its fans, and a site that resembles its rivals makes it hard to see why it is the better choice. Being different is less risky than looking the same; a strong response, even a mixed one, beats indifference, and having some people dislike the brand shows it has a point of view.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, text
- **Sources:** GENTRY-018 (page 29-30, the notes cite 30), WALTER-082 (page 92)
- **Also in (not read):** WALTER-022, WALTER-043
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate trust E54

### trust-033 · Let the people behind the company show
- **Statement:** Let the brand's personality show clearly so the audience can relate to the company as to a person: a business is a group of people, and users want to connect with real people. Design the interface as if a person, not a machine, is on the other side: the computer recedes and the personalities of the people behind it come forward, as in a good conversation. A site that shows a human company with its customers' interests at heart can convince prospects before they ever make contact.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, text
- **Sources:** WALTER-013 (page 23), WALTER-088 (page 98), WALTER-025 (page 38)

### trust-038 · A domain name that is easy to remember and to say
- **Statement:** The domain name is the most important part of a URL: users who remember it reach the home page and can search or navigate from there. ### trust-039 · The country's domain for a local site, .com for a worldwide English one
- **Statement:** An English site used worldwide should take a .com domain. A site in any other language, or one serving mainly local visitors, local events or local products, should use its country's domain whatever its language, because people expect local services under the local domain and .com can mislead them.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look
- **Sources:** NIELSEN-531 (page 402)
- **Asked of the source:** that visitors still read the domain ending as a sign of where a site serves, and what current guidance says on choosing a country domain.
- **Current source:** confirmed by https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites (read 2026-11-20)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate trust E72

### trust-040 · A default serves the person, not the provider
- **Statement:** Preticked boxes in a software install serve two ends: sparing users a hard choice, and serving the supplier (consent to emails about new products); defaults sometimes reflect someone else's preferences, and not all of them are there to help. A default chosen to protect the provider from lawsuits is as irresponsible as a reckless one: the book's case is pension plans that made the most cautious investment the default.
- **Kind:** practice
- **Use:** look, check
- **Sources:** THALER-099 (page 84-85), THALER-149 (page 118)
- **Read:** 2026-11-20, page, Opus agent, each part checked by Claude; candidate forms E107

### trust-043 · Persuade, do not deceive
- **Statement:** Persuasion is acceptable when it is not deceptive; manipulation is not, from a half-hidden checkbox ticked by default to sign people up for a newsletter, to adverts styled like the site's own download button while the real message sits in one line of small pale grey text, up to phishing.

- **Use:** look, check

- **Use:** text
